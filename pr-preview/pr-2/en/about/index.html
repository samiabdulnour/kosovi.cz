About

Daniela and Martin Kos

Daniela and Martin Kos have worked in their Prague studio for decades. Their work ranges from tableware to art objects for indoor and outdoor spaces. People know them above all for their blue and white porcelain, which is in use in many homes in the Czech Republic and abroad. Every piece is thrown and painted by hand, so no two are quite alike.

Martin is a trained potter. He has stayed with the potter’s wheel since childhood and works mostly in porcelain. Daniela often draws on her fairy-tale imagination. Her works come with drawing or colour. She uses many different techniques in her work.

Blue and white porcelain

All the blue and white pieces are thrown from technical porcelain. It was first used for electrical insulators, which stand outdoors in harsh conditions all year round.

The material is safe for food, but it is harder to work than ordinary porcelain or pottery.

Every piece is thrown on the potter’s wheel and fired twice. After the first firing in an electric kiln, it is painted by hand with wax and then dipped in cobalt, which gives it its blue colour. Then it is dipped in a clear glaze, which keeps the decoration from wearing off. In the second, high firing at 1,380 °C in a gas kiln, all the layers fuse.

The result is a fused, durable body. The pieces can go in the dishwasher and the microwave, and you can use them as an oven dish too.

Daniela Kosová

I was born in Bratislava. Since childhood I have loved drawing and modelling. In my home town I studied ceramics at the secondary school of applied arts, and then went on to UMPRUM in Prague. I consider the sculpture and ceramics training in Andrej Rudavský’s studio and the practice in the porcelain factories in Dubí and Duchcov essential to my artistic education.

My greatest inspiration is porcelain. It is a very delicate, sensitive and beautiful material. Working with it is technically very demanding, but it offers many possibilities in both shaping and decoration.

I decorate with various techniques: underglaze, fired at 1,390 °C, in-glaze, fired at 1,250 °C, and overglaze including gilding (fired at 800 °C). I love this work and I am grateful for it.

My work can be found in the collections of the Museum of Decorative Arts in Prague, the Aleš South Bohemian Gallery, the Slovak Design Centre in Bratislava, the museum in Darney in France, Porvoo in Finland, and in many private collections at home and abroad.

Martin Kos

I was born in 1959 in Hradec Králové. My parents, both ceramicists, led me to love the craft when I was still a schoolboy. In the holidays I went to a pottery workshop in Týniště nad Orlicí, where I later trained as a potter. Then I graduated from the ceramics school in Bechyně, like my parents and even my grandfather.

After school I worked at the Keramo Praha arts and crafts cooperative, and in 1987 I began working on my own. I have always wanted to make things whose craftsmanship my grandfather and the other old masters I met in my training and at pottery competitions would praise.

In time I learned to throw porcelain on the wheel too, and once I knew its advantages over pottery, I never went back. For more than 25 years I have worked only in porcelain. I have learned that it is very demanding to work, but what is made from it is strong and durable.

People do not buy what I make because they need it to live, but mainly because they like it and it has proved itself in everyday use. That pleases me and drives me to keep improving. There is always more to learn and always room to improve.
